## Executive summary

Overall risk posture is **High** for financial completeness. Role and hub checks, transaction boundaries, cashbook-day checks, split-wallet journals, and reasoned OS reversals are meaningful positive controls. However, the rider settlement model cannot accept a second payment for a partially paid day, while the remaining balance is date-scoped and disappears from the next day's default dashboard. Separately, expected remittance is recalculated from mutable current records rather than an immutable delivery obligation snapshot. These defects can strand receivables and change historical amounts owed.

Finding counts: **Critical 0, High 2, Medium 4, Low 0**.

## Findings

### AUD-20260813-01 — High — Partial rider remittances strand the remaining receivable

**Location:** `backend/src/services/finance.service.ts:264-350, 995-1026`; `backend/prisma/schema.prisma:364-376`; `mobile/app/(tabs)/settlement.tsx:20-33`; `backend/src/services/master-data.service.ts:229-244,264-280`

**Evidence:** Outstanding is calculated only for one requested business date as `expectedAmount - settlement.actualAmount`. `Settlement` is unique on `(riderId, businessDate)`, and `createRiderSettlement` rejects every later payment after the first settlement. The Rider app locks all declaration inputs whenever any settlement exists, even when `outstandingAmount > 0`. Dashboard totals request only today's business date.

**Failure scenario:** A rider owes 400,000 MMK and pays 300,000 MMK. The system posts a 100,000 variance/receivable, but tomorrow the normal dashboard shows today's riders only, and neither Finance nor the rider can post the remaining 100,000 against the original obligation. The balance is visible only by manually revisiting the original date and remains operationally uncollectable in the workflow.

**Impact:** Rider receivables can disappear from daily attention, settlements cannot be completed through multiple payments, and wallet/cashbook records require unrelated adjustments to represent later collection.

**Mitigation:** Model an immutable rider settlement obligation plus append-only payment/remittance records. Allow multiple idempotent payments across Cash/KBZ/Wave until the obligation reaches zero; carry all open balances into current outstanding queries/dashboard and Rider app; retain original business date and payment date separately. Do not lock the Rider screen merely because one payment exists.

### AUD-20260813-02 — High — Historical rider amount owed is recalculated from mutable current state

**Location:** `backend/src/services/finance.service.ts:187-260, 1027-1038`

**Evidence:** Settlement work selects parcels that are currently `DELIVERED`, reads current `Parcel.codAmount`, `Parcel.deliveryFee`, current linked-group fee/status, and current Rider pay model/rate/salary. Only individual non-linked commission preferentially reads a persisted `DeliveryWay.commissionAmount`. Both preview and posting recalculate this work at request time.

**Failure scenario:** After delivery but before evening settlement, an authorized rider pay configuration change or linked-group/fee change alters the amount the rider owes for an already completed delivery. A linked group becoming incomplete can also remove fees/commission from the calculation even though another member's delivery obligation was already recognized.

**Impact:** Historical obligations are not reproducible, riders may be over/under charged, and the ledger can disagree with delivery-time commercial terms.

**Mitigation:** At delivery, persist an immutable per-obligation snapshot containing rider, COD collected, fee collected, commission, salary policy/effective rate, linked-group allocation, and recognition timestamp. Settlement must sum open snapshot obligations, never current master/parcel configuration. Corrections should be explicit reversal/adjustment events.

### AUD-20260813-03 — Medium — OS idempotency replay does not compare financial payload

**Location:** `backend/src/services/finance.service.ts:478-485`

**Evidence:** An existing idempotency key is considered equivalent when shop, business date, wallet, and batch IDs match. It does not compare `hubId`, advance deduction, return deduction, delivery-fee deduction, adjustment amount, or adjustment reason.

**Failure scenario:** A timed-out client retries the same key after an operator edits a deduction or adjustment. The API returns the earlier settlement as successful instead of reporting that the key was reused with different financial intent.

**Impact:** The UI/operator may believe revised figures were posted, producing silent statement mismatch.

**Mitigation:** Store a canonical request hash or compare every normalized financial and scope field on replay. Return `409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT` for any mismatch.

### AUD-20260813-04 — Medium — Database does not enforce one active OS settlement per batch

**Location:** `backend/prisma/schema.prisma:417-429`; `backend/prisma/migrations/20260813113000_os_settlement_workspace/migration.sql`; `backend/src/services/finance.service.ts:498-516`

**Evidence:** `OsSettlementBatch` is unique only on `(settlementId, batchId)` and merely indexed by `batchId`. Eligibility is enforced by an application read followed by inserts in a serializable transaction.

**Failure scenario:** Concurrency behavior differs between SQLite and production PostgreSQL, or a future/import path inserts links without the eligibility check. The database permits two posted settlements to reference the same batch.

**Impact:** Duplicate shop payments and duplicated settlement statements remain possible if transactional assumptions or code paths regress.

**Mitigation:** Encode the invariant in storage. Options include a batch-level active settlement pointer with a unique constraint, or an active-claim table whose batch ID is unique and is released/marked on reversal. Add a real concurrent-post integration test against PostgreSQL.

### AUD-20260813-05 — Medium — Dashboard finance totals can include the wrong event classes and are inconsistent by role

**Location:** `backend/src/services/master-data.service.ts:231-284`

**Evidence:** For scoped users, candidate source IDs are narrowed to Parcel, linked group, or Rider `Settlement`; OS settlement IDs are excluded. For Superadmin, `hubId` is undefined and no source-type narrowing occurs, so OS settlement journal lines participate. An OS settlement debits `OS_COD_PAYABLE` and credits `DELIVERY_FEE_REVENUE`; these can reduce “COD collected today” and count delivery fees again for Superadmin. Superadmin totals also aggregate every hub with no selectable hub context.

**Failure scenario:** The same business data yields different revenue/COD metrics depending on whether Finance or Superadmin views it, and posting an OS settlement changes a card labelled customer collections even though no customer collection occurred.

**Impact:** Management dashboard figures can be materially misleading and cannot reliably reconcile to event-specific reports.

**Mitigation:** Aggregate each metric from explicit source types/accounts and recognition events, always scope by an explicit hub or present a clearly labelled organization aggregate, and add role-parity/reconciliation tests including OS settlement and reversal days.

### AUD-20260813-06 — Medium — Outstanding/dashboard queries scale as N+1 and load full parcel graphs

**Location:** `backend/src/services/finance.service.ts:297-350`; `backend/src/services/master-data.service.ts:234-244`

**Evidence:** `listRiderOutstanding` loads all active riders, then performs settlement work plus declaration and settlement queries per rider. Settlement work loads every delivered parcel, every way, and every linked group's parcels. Dashboard invokes this full routine on every load.

**Failure scenario:** Rider and parcel growth turns one dashboard request into hundreds of queries and large nested reads, increasing latency and database contention at evening settlement—the highest-concurrency operational period.

**Impact:** Slow dashboards, connection-pool exhaustion, and settlement-time reliability degradation.

**Mitigation:** Query only riders with open obligations, aggregate immutable obligation/payment rows in SQL by rider, batch-fetch declarations/settlements, paginate detail endpoints, and cache/recompute dashboard summaries by hub/business date.

## Performance, reliability, and scalability observations

- OS posting/reversal uses serializable transactions and checks cashbook closure inside the transaction. The reversal journal swaps every original debit/credit and the unique journal `(sourceType, sourceId)` key helps reject duplicate reversals.
- OS settlement list/draft endpoints cap draft batches at 200 and posted settlements currently have no pagination; the latter should be paginated before long-term production use.
- Money is represented as integers, but request validators do not impose a safe upper bound. Add a documented maximum below JavaScript's safe integer and database/accounting limits.
- The phone helper strips formatting and checks platform capability before opening `tel:`. Township sorting/filtering is local, deterministic, and covered by focused tests. No direct phone or cross-rider data exposure was found in these helpers; server-side assigned-parcel ownership remains the critical control.

## Positive controls verified

- Finance actors are reloaded from the database and checked for active status, matching role, and hub scope.
- Rider preview/declaration resolves the authenticated Rider to their own profile; a Rider cannot request another rider ID.
- Rider settlement records Cash, KBZ Pay, and Wave Pay as separate settlement and wallet journal lines.
- Wallet mismatch requires a reason and declaration verification uses an optimistic `updatedAt` predicate.
- Rider and OS posting checks that the cashbook day is open inside the transaction.
- OS posting deduplicates batch IDs, verifies shop/hub ownership, rejects unresolved batches and already-posted active links, and uses a client idempotency key.
- OS reversal is limited to Superadmin/Finance, enforces hub scope, requires a reason, preserves the original settlement, and writes a compensating journal.
- Constructed rider and OS journal formulas inspected were arithmetically balanced for positive, zero, and negative variance/net cases.
- Rider mobile clearly distinguishes declaration from hub verification and exposes the split wallet mix, outstanding amount, township filtering/sorting, and guarded phone launch.
