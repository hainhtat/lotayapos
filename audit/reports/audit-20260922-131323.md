## Final isolation verdict

### Save-versus-finalize snapshot race — Resolved

**Locations:** `backend/src/services/operations.service.ts:94-105`, `:360-423`; `backend/src/services/os-account.service.ts:55-91`; `backend/src/services/finance/cashbook-policy.ts:5-47`; regression coverage at `backend/tests/tracking-allocation.test.ts:103-123`.

PostgreSQL finalization now uses `READ COMMITTED` after taking the transaction-level per-batch advisory lock as its first operation. Under this isolation level, the batch query following a blocked lock acquisition receives a fresh statement snapshot and therefore observes the preceding parcel-save transaction. This directly prevents the observed stale obligation (`7001`) versus saved COD (`7751`) mismatch.

The lower isolation level does not remove required financial serialization:

- parcel bulk creation, financial parcel edits, finalization, and the legacy pickup-advance path use the same per-batch mutation lock;
- finalization holds that lock through the finalized marker, obligation creation, journal posting, credit allocation, and commit;
- OS credit allocation additionally uses a `(shopId, hubId)` advisory lock;
- cashbook access additionally uses a `(hubId, businessDate)` advisory lock and rechecks the day while holding it;
- uniqueness constraints and replay branches continue to prevent duplicate obligation, allocation, carry-forward credit, and journal records.

SQLite remains `SERIALIZABLE`, appropriate because it does not use the PostgreSQL advisory-lock mechanism and serializes writes at database level.

No lock-order cycle was identified in the inspected flow: finalization acquires batch, then OS-credit, then cashbook locks; parcel save/edit uses the batch lock, and cashbook close uses the cashbook lock without attempting to acquire a batch or OS-credit lock.
